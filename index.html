Strata for Mac

System Data, taken apart layer by layer.

Settings › Storage shows one grey bar called System Data and a number. Strata splits your disk into named layers — developer caches, simulator runtimes, diagnostic reports, old downloads — explains each one in plain words, and helps you clean only what can safely go.

Free on the Mac App Store. Requires macOS 26 or later.

Scan. Understand. Clean.

  1. 01

    Scan

    The first time you open Strata, it shows Allow Strata to Read Your Disk. Click Choose Macintosh HD…: the window opens at the top of your disk, Macintosh HD, so just click Allow. Strata then measures what each folder really takes up and shows it as a disk map you can walk down by size.

  2. 02

    Understand

    Every item comes with a short explanation: what created it, what happens if it goes, and how you would get it back. A grade shows at a glance what regenerates and what is better left alone.

  3. 03

    Clean

    Files you clean are moved to the Trash, so you can put them back. Items that a developer tool has to remove itself, such as Homebrew caches or simulator runtimes, can’t be checked in the App Store version. Instead, the copy button in the inspector on the right copies that tool’s own command for you to run in Terminal.

Four grades, decided before you click anything.

Every item Strata recognises has a grade in its rule set. The grade decides what Strata will offer to do with it.

Safe

Caches and downloads that apps and tools rebuild when they need them.

Homebrew downloads, the Swift package cache, npm caches.

Review

Fine to remove, but you will have to download it again if you need it. Strata lists each item so you can choose.

Simulator runtimes, Android system images, Xcode device support files.

Protected

Contains your data. Strata explains it and shows where it is, but leaves the decision to you and the app that owns it.

App data in Application Support, message attachments.

System

Parts of macOS that Strata does not clean. It explains what they are and why they grow; depending on what macOS lets the App Store version see, it shows their size or marks them as not measured. Most shrink by themselves after an update or a restart.

Strata explains local snapshots, swap and a staged macOS update.

Nothing leaves your Mac.

Strata collects no data. It has no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not talk to any server. The only network activity is Apple’s own StoreKit, when you buy or restore the purchase.

Scans read only file information such as names, sizes and dates. Strata compares file contents only when you start Find Duplicates yourself, and it does so on this Mac without sending anything anywhere.

Scan results and your cleanup history are stored only on this Mac. Updates arrive through the Mac App Store. Read the privacy policy.
